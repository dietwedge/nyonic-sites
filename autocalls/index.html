The problem

Every ring is a bay you have not filled yet.

A repair shop's phone rings hardest between eight and ten, which is exactly when every tech is turning wrenches and the person who answers is also the person writing the tickets.

So it rings out, or somebody picks up mid-job with their hands full and takes half a message. The call does not go unanswered because you stopped caring. It goes unanswered because you were under a car.

The caller is not waiting. They ring the next shop, and the shop that answered gets the tow in, the diagnosis, and the brake job behind it.

What one unanswered morning costs

A diagnostic is an hour of labour. The repair behind it is a day's ticket, and the customer who got a real answer at eight in the morning brings you the other car too.

You are not being undercut. You are being out-answered.

It asks what you would ask

Every call, the same questions you would ask.

Takes the year, make, model and what it is doing before it books anything. These are the questions it actually works through — not a summary of them.

  • Year, make and model, and roughly the mileage
  • What it is doing, in their words: a noise, a warning light, will not start, overheating, pulling, leaking
  • Whether it is drivable right now, and whether it is somewhere safe
  • Whether it is already at the shop, at home, or needs to be towed in
  • Whether they need it back by a particular day, and whether they need a ride or a loaner
  • Whether this is a warranty, an insurance claim or a fleet account, because that changes who authorises the work

What it isn't

No chatbot on your website.

A chat bubble is the most visible and least useful version of this. Somebody with a real problem at nine at night is not typing at you. They are calling.

It says it's automated. On every call, in the first sentence. Pretending a machine is you works exactly once, and the moment somebody notices you have spent the trust this was supposed to earn.

It never quotes a price. It gathers, it books, and it hands over. Anything that commits you to a number or a promise routes to a human first.

Questions

The ones everybody asks.

Will my customers know it's a machine?

Yes, and it tells them — it says it is the automated line in its first sentence, on every call. It sounds like a calm person who is good at their job, and it does not need to pretend. The caller wanted somebody to pick up.

What if it can't handle the call?

It says so and gets you. An emergency, a complaint, or a price it should not guess is handed to a person along with notes from the call. Handing off well is a success, not a failure.

Do I have to change my phone system or software?

No. Keep your number and point your overflow at us — after hours, at weekends, or after a set number of rings. Nothing on your phone changes and there is no new app to remember to open.

How long until it's running?

About a week, and most of that is writing your questions with you. The answering itself works on day one.

What happens to the recordings?

Recording is off unless you switch it on, and when it is on the notice plays before the greeting rather than after it. Recordings are yours, they are never sent to a customer, and you can delete them.

How do I know it's actually working?

A summary text every week: what came in, what got booked, what needed you. Every call is logged with what it asked and what it did, and you can export the lot whenever you want.
