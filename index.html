The problem

Great tournaments are held together by too many tabs.

A bracket site, a Discord, a roster sheet, a stream doc, an incident file, and a folder of result screenshots. You become the thing keeping them in sync, and every hop between them is somewhere a mistake gets in.

The toolkit

Eleven modules. One window.

Each one does a job you are already doing by hand. Nothing here is a dashboard for its own sake.

01

Tournament builder

Name, date, format and match length. Single elimination, double elimination or round robin — the choice drives the whole event.

02

Team + player manager

Rosters with starters and substitutes, captains, orgs and logos. Search finds a team by any player's gamertag.

03

Bracket engine

Byes handled automatically. Correct a result and every later round un-advances with it, because the bracket is recomputed rather than pushed forward.

04

Match control

Add a game per map won. The match closes itself at the right score and advances the winner.

05

Check-in Now online

Captains check themselves in from a private link — on your network, or from anywhere once you publish it.

06

Rules center

A versioned rulebook. Publishing a new version clears acceptances, because a rule that changed was not the one they agreed to.

07

Broadcast center

An OBS browser source with real transparency, a run of show, and caster notes.

08

Live scoreboard New

A public page for spectators showing fixtures, scores and the champion. No rosters, no names, no lobby codes.

09

AI caster notes New

Talking points drawn from what actually happened — records, earlier meetings, win streaks. Runs on your own API key, cents per event.

10

Results + awards

Standings, an event page, roster CSV, and certificates as a real PDF.

11

Event backups

Every change written atomically with a rolling backup. If the power goes, the event does not.
