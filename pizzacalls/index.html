The problem

You don't lose those jobs on price. You lose them on silence.

A pizzeria's phone problem is not that the calls stop. It is that they all arrive in the same ninety minutes, on the night when every person in the building already has both hands full.

Somebody has to come off the make line, wipe their hands, and pick up — to answer that you close at ten, that yes you deliver to that street, that it is about thirty-five minutes right now. Then do it again. The call does not go unanswered because you stopped caring. It goes unanswered because you were making pizza.

And the one call you truly could not afford to miss — the eight-pie office order for Thursday — sounded exactly like the other forty while it was ringing.

What one missed Friday costs

A dropped Friday call is one order. A dropped catering call is a standing weekly order and the office that keeps ordering from whoever picked up first.

And a phone order is worth more to you than the same order through a delivery app that takes a third of it.

What it isn't

No chatbot on your website.

A chat bubble is the most visible and least useful version of this. The person who wants a pizza in thirty minutes is not typing at you. They are calling.

It says it's automated. On every call, in the first sentence. Pretending a machine is you works exactly once, and the moment somebody notices, you have spent the trust this was supposed to earn.

It never quotes a catering price or takes payment. It gathers the order and hands it over. Anything that commits you to a number, a delivery time you cannot make, or a card on file routes to a human first.

Questions

The ones everybody asks.

Will my customers know it's a machine?

Yes, because it says so in the first sentence of every call. That is deliberate. Speed is what you are selling here, not the illusion of a person. In practice callers do not mind at all — being answered at 9pm beats a voicemail box read on Monday.

What if it can't handle the call?

It hands off. A complaint about an order, anything unusual, anything outside what we set up — it goes to a real person, or gets flagged for a callback with the recording attached. It is built to know its edges.

Do I have to change my phone system or software?

No. You keep your number, your POS and your delivery apps. This sits behind the phone line as the overflow destination — the same slot ringing out occupies now.

How long until it's running?

About a week. Most of that is the two of us going through your menu, your hours and your delivery zone, which is the part that decides whether any of it is any good.

What happens to the recordings?

They are yours. You get every call, every transcript, and the customer list, exported whenever you ask. Nothing about this is designed to make leaving difficult.

How do I know it's actually working?

We agree the number before anything is built — calls answered during the rush, orders taken without anybody leaving the line, catering enquiries captured. If that number hasn't moved in the first month, we change it or you switch it off. I would rather lose the work than keep invoicing for something that isn't landing.
